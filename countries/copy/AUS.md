## How they vote

Australia's 2025 federal election, held on 3 May 2025, filled all 150 seats in the House of Representatives using preferential (instant-runoff) voting. Voters rank candidates, and preferences are counted until one candidate holds a majority. In the Senate, 40 of 76 seats were also contested. Australian citizens aged 18 and over must enrol and vote, and voting is compulsory: missing a vote without a valid reason can bring a $20 penalty, according to the Australian Electoral Commission (AEC). Labor won 94 of the 150 House seats (AEC). Turnout for the House was 90.7%, with 16,409,748 votes from 18,091,591 enrolled electors, according to the AEC.

## Wild facts

- Voting is compulsory, and a citizen who fails to vote without a valid reason faces a $20 penalty. (https://www.aec.gov.au/about_aec/publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm)
- 56% of votes (9,164,455) were cast before election day. (https://www.aec.gov.au/Elections/federal_elections/2025/2025-Federal-Election-Report.pdf)
- Bradfield was the most marginal seat, won by an independent (AEC). (https://www.aec.gov.au/Elections/federal_elections/2025/2025-Federal-Election-Report.pdf)
- More than 1,700 polling places offered a "democracy sausage," a sausage sizzle usually run to raise money for schools or clubs, according to democracysausage.org as reported by Nine.
