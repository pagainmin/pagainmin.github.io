## How they vote

The United States held midterm elections on 3 November 2026 for all 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats, about one-third of the Senate. Most contests use first-past-the-post in single-member districts, with further rules set by each state. US citizens aged 18 and over can vote, under the 26th Amendment, and voting is not compulsory. As of early 4 November, AP results showed Democrats with 228 House seats and Republicans 139, with 68 uncalled. In the Senate, Democrats held 54 seats (including 2 independents) and Republicans 44, with Alaska and Mississippi uncalled.

## Wild facts

- In the 2024 presidential election, 65.3% of the citizen voting-age population voted, according to the Census Bureau. (https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/2024-presidential-election-voting-registration-tables.html)
- In 2024, adults under 30 made up 15% of voters but about 20% of the eligible population, according to Pew Research Center. (https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/06/26/voter-turnout-2020-2024/)
- In Gallup's survey, 49% of Americans said they were confident in the honesty of elections in 2026, down from 51% in 2024. (https://news.gallup.com/poll/715205/trust-elections-breaks-global-pattern.aspx)
